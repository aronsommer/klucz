Alfabet i wymowa – Alphabet and Pronunciation

Stress is almost always on the second-last syllable: Polska · Warszawa · dziękuję

1. Alphabet · 32 letters, bold = not in English

A Ą B C Ć D E Ę F G H I J K L Ł M N Ń O Ó P R S Ś T U W Y Z Ź Ż

q, v, x only in foreign words · Almost everything is pronounced the way it is written.

2. Vowels · sound roughly like …

Letter Pronunciation Example
e always open, like e in “bed” nie – no, not
o always open, like o in “hot” dom – house
ó = u both like oo in “book” góra – mountain · ulica – street
y a dark i, a bit like i in “bit” ryba – fish
ą nasal o są – they are
ę nasal e; at the end mostly plain e ręka – hand · idę → “ide”

3. Consonants · different from English

ł like w in “water” mały – small
w like v in “very” woda – water
z like z in “zoo” zupa – soup
s always like s in “sun” sok – juice
c like ts in “cats” noc – night
ch = h both like ch in “loch” chleb – bread · herbata – tea
r rolled with the tongue tip rower – bicycle

4. Hissing sounds · hard and soft

si, ci, zi, dzi, ni = ś, ć, ź, dź, ń · before a vowel the i only makes it soft: siedem = “ś-edem”

s sounds hardsimilar to English sh softlike hard, but softer
s · sok sz like sh · szkoła ś / si · środa, siedem
c · noc cz like ch · czas ć / ci · być, ciocia
z · zupa ż = rz like s in “pleasure” · żona ź / zi · źle, zima
dz · dzwon dż like j in “jam” · dżem dź / dzi · dzień

Remember

ń and ni sound like ny in “canyon” · koń (horse), nie

Hard at the end of a word: b → p, d → t, g → k, w → f, z → s, ż → sz · chleb → “chlep” · Kraków → “Krakuf”

rz after p, t, k, ch sounds like sz · przepraszam → “pszepraszam” · trzy → “tszy”
